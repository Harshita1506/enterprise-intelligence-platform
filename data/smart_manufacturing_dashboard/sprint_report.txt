1. Sprint Executive Summary
Sprint 9 was a tale of two environments. The frontend and database teams achieved significant wins, successfully deploying the global OEE dashboard for the Texas plant with sub-second query times. However, the Operational Technology (OT) and data ingestion teams hit a major brick wall. Integrating the legacy equipment at the Ohio plant overwhelmed our WAN bandwidth and cloud Kafka clusters with noisy vibration data. We have had to execute a mid-sprint architectural pivot toward heavy Edge Computing, which has caused all Ohio-related integration tickets to spill over.

5. Current Blockers & Issues

Ohio Bandwidth Limitations (Critical Blocker): The Ohio plant's network cannot handle streaming raw, uncompressed 1000Hz sensor data. This caused massive packet loss and crashed our Kafka brokers. We are completely blocked on Ohio until industrial Edge servers are procured and installed to aggregate the data locally.

Procurement Lead Times: The Dell Edge Gateways we need to solve the bandwidth issue have a 4-week lead time, pushing the Ohio Go-Live date out by a minimum of one month.

6. Technical Debt & Quality Notes

Kafka Auto-Scaling: The sprint exposed a flaw in our Amazon MSK auto-scaling configuration. When Ohio flooded the broker, it did not scale storage quickly enough, resulting in dropped partitions. Priya Patel has added a high-priority ticket to Sprint 10 to adjust the scaling thresholds.

OT/IT security policies are slowing down deployment. Because engineers cannot remote into the factory floor, Michael Chen loses 2 days to travel every time a legacy PLC requires manual configuration.
